# Presentation Script Template — the 90-Second Five-Beat

A standard, repeatable structure for presenting a news article to the
class. Short and sweet: about 90 seconds of talking, one slide, five
beats. Fill in the blanks in your own words — if a sentence doesn't
sound like something you'd actually say out loud, rewrite it until it
does.

**Slide rule:** the slide is your map, not your script. Each beat below
points at one part of the slide; you talk, the slide backs you up.
Never read the slide to the room — they can read.

---

## Beat 1 — The Hook (≈10 seconds)
*Look at the class, not the screen. One or two sentences on why YOU
picked this.*

> "I chose this article because ______________________________."

*Good hooks are personal ("I'm in Animation III, so this one's
basically about my future job") or surprising (lead with the wildest
fact).*

## Beat 2 — What Happened (≈30 seconds)
*Point at the slide's main bullets. Three facts, your own words —
this is your summary out loud, not read off the page.*

> "So here's the story: ______________________________.
> ______________________________. And ______________________________."

## Beat 3 — The Number (≈10 seconds)
*Point at the stat callout on the slide. Give the number room —
pause after it.*

> "Here's the stat that got me: ______." *(pause)*
> "That means ______________________________."

## Beat 4 — Why It Matters (≈15 seconds)
*Connect it to your audience — teenagers in Southern California.
If it continues a story from an earlier week, say so here.*

> "This matters to us because ______________________________."

## Beat 5 — Your Questions (≈20 seconds)
*The two questions from your write-up. Throw them at the room and
actually wait — the silence works for you.*

> "So I'll leave you with two questions: ______________________?
> And ______________________?"

*Close by citing the source out loud:*

> "This was from ____________, published ____________."

---

## Delivery checklist

- [ ] Sounds like me talking to friends, not an essay read aloud
- [ ] Slide has the same beats I'm saying (title, 3 bullets, stat, questions)
- [ ] Practiced aloud once; 60-second phone recording made Wednesday,
      watched once, fixed the one thing that bugged me
- [ ] Eyes on the class for the hook and the questions
- [ ] Under two minutes — short and confident beats long and rambling
