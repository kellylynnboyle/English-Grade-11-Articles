## Summary

In an MIT News article published August 12, 2026, science writer Jennifer
Chu reports on the discovery of an entirely new class of cosmic object,
nicknamed a "black hole star." Using the James Webb Space Telescope,
astronomers identified an object called MoM-BH*-1, whose light dates back
to roughly 660 million years after the Big Bang (Chu). Unlike a normal
star, which is powered by nuclear fusion, this object is a massive,
actively feeding black hole wrapped in such a dense envelope of hydrogen
gas that the whole system mimics the spectral signature of a giant star.
It is roughly the size of our solar system but radiates about 100 billion
times more energy than an ordinary star (Chu). Researchers believe this
newly identified type of object may finally explain the many mysterious
"little red dots" that JWST has been spotting throughout images of the
early universe — compact, extremely bright, red-tinted points that
scientists had struggled to categorize.

## Annotations

- MoM-BH*-1: light traveled ~660 million years after the Big Bang to
  reach JWST — an extremely early-universe object.
- Powered by active black hole accretion, not fusion — a genuinely new
  category, not just an extreme version of a known object.
- Size comparable to our solar system; energy output ~100 billion times
  greater than a typical star.
- Discovery published in the peer-reviewed journal *Nature*, Aug. 12,
  2026 — same date as the MIT News writeup.
- Significance: may resolve the "little red dots" puzzle that has
  persisted since JWST began imaging the deep early universe.

## Personal Response & Questions

What strikes me most is that this isn't just a new record-holder (biggest,
oldest, brightest) — it's a genuinely new category of object that didn't
fit any existing physics model. It makes me think about how much of what
JWST is finding might not be "extreme versions" of things we already
understand, but things we don't have a name for yet.

1. If "black hole stars" like MoM-BH*-1 explain many of the "little red
   dots" JWST keeps finding, how many other unexplained objects in deep
   space might turn out to be the same kind of hybrid object?
2. How do astronomers actually distinguish a black hole star's light
   signature from an ordinary distant galaxy's light when both can appear
   as just a small red dot from so far away?
