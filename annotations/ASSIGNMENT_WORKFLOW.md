# Weekly News Article Annotation — Assignment Workflow

This is the working version of the teacher's assignment instructions,
reorganized into a step-by-step checklist. The grading rubric and
requirements below are the teacher's and are not to be altered — only the
presentation is enhanced for clarity and to guide topic selection. This file
is the reference for this recurring routine; `TOPIC_PREFERENCES.md` in this
same folder governs which subject area to pull from each week.

## Original assignment (verbatim, for reference)

For all news article annotation assignments this year, use the same format
and steps:

1. Find a current news article that relates to world or local news. NOT a
   celebrity fluff piece or sports article.
2. Read your article at least one time.
3. Read your article again and begin your annotation process. Use the name
   of the article and news source where you found it as your summary title.
4. Find the most important and relevant information from the article to
   annotate. There is no right/wrong answer.
5. Compose a brief summary of the article utilizing the information/details
   you find most important. Your summary must be AT LEAST one paragraph and
   be in MLA format.
6. Add some of your own thoughts, concerns, questions, suggestions, ideas to
   your annotation summary. You must include AT LEAST 2 questions that you
   have about your news article.
7. You must annotate your article fully. Most annotation summaries will
   require you to write more than a paragraph.
8. You will present your annotation summary and questions to the class at
   the end of each week.
9. Submit your summary by attaching your Google Doc to the assignment in
   Schoology. You also need to submit a hard copy of the article that has
   been annotated. Your Google Doc submission needs to be in MLA format.
10. This assignment is worth 10 points each week: 5 points for the
    presentation, 3 points for the summary/personal questions, 2 points for
    the annotations.

## Weekly checklist (working version)

**Step 1 — Pick the article.**
Source a current (this week's) world or local news article. No celebrity
gossip, no sports. Per `TOPIC_PREFERENCES.md`, favor articles that connect
to one of this year's classes — Animation, Physics, Algebra 2, or Sign
Language — and rotate which class's subject area gets used so the same one
doesn't repeat two weeks running. Present a few options and let the student
pick before drafting anything.

**Step 2 — First read.**
Read the full article once, straight through, no annotating yet — just for
overall understanding.

**Step 3 — Second read + annotate.**
Read again. This pass is where the annotating happens: underline/highlight
and margin-note the details that matter as you go. Title the summary
document with the article's name and the news source (e.g. *"Article Title"
— Source Name*).

**Step 4 — Pull the key info.**
From the second read, pick out the most important facts, figures, quotes,
and context — who/what/when/where/why. No single right answer; choose what
you judge most important.

**Step 5 — Write the summary (MLA format).**
At least one full paragraph, MLA-formatted:
- Header: student name, teacher name, course/period, date (upper left,
  double-spaced) — or an MLA heading block if the teacher prefers that
  layout.
- 12 pt Times New Roman, double-spaced, 1" margins.
- Works Cited entry for the article at the end.
- In-text citation when quoting or closely paraphrasing.

**Step 6 — Add personal response.**
Include your own thoughts, concerns, or ideas about the article, and AT
LEAST 2 original questions it raised for you. These should be genuine
questions — things you'd actually want answered, not rhetorical ones.

**Step 7 — Confirm full annotation.**
Check that the annotations are thorough — most weeks this means more than
one paragraph of margin notes/highlights, not just the summary paragraph.

**Step 8 — Prep to present.**
Be ready to present the summary and questions to the class at the end of
the week (this is worth half the weekly grade — 5 of 10 points).

**Step 9 — Submit.**
- Attach the MLA-formatted Google Doc to the Schoology assignment.
- Submit a hard copy of the annotated article itself (the physical/printed
  markup, separate from the Google Doc).

**Step 10 — Grading (for reference).**
10 points/week: 5 presentation, 3 summary + personal questions, 2
annotations.

## MLA quick reference

**Works Cited (online news article):**
`Author Last, First (or organization/wire service if no byline). "Article
Title." *Publication Name*, Day Month Year, URL.`

**In-text citation:** `(Author Last Name)` or, if no named author,
`("Short Title")`.
